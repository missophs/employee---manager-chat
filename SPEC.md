# Performance Pulse — Product Specification

A private, interactive performance and 1:1 platform designed exclusively for employees and
their direct managers. It replaces static performance-review forms with an ongoing,
conversational experience.

## Purpose

Help employees and managers:

- Prepare for meaningful 1:1 conversations
- Discuss performance and expectations
- Set and track goals
- Give and receive feedback
- Identify obstacles and support needs
- Create learning and development plans
- Discuss career growth
- Document agreements and follow-up actions
- Prepare for performance reviews
- Maintain a history of meaningful conversations
- Export selected information when they intentionally choose to share it with HR

## Critical privacy principle

Only the employee and their manager have access to information within the platform.

- HR does not have automatic access.
- There is no HR dashboard, HR portal, automatic HR reporting, or automatic sharing.
- If an employee or manager wants HR to see something, they intentionally export the
  relevant information into a file and provide it to HR themselves.
- The platform must clearly communicate this privacy model to users.

---

## 1. User roles

Two roles only.

### Employee

Complete self-reflections · prepare for 1:1s · add discussion topics · add personal goals ·
track progress · request feedback · provide feedback to their manager · identify development
goals · suggest learning opportunities · discuss career aspirations · create action items ·
view conversation history · export their information.

### Manager

Prepare for 1:1s · add discussion topics · provide feedback · recognize achievements ·
discuss performance concerns · set and review goals · suggest learning and development ·
recommend stretch assignments · support career development · create action items · view the
employee's shared information · view conversation history · export information when needed.

### No HR role

Do not build an HR user role. Do not create an HR dashboard. Do not automatically send
information to HR. Do not automatically notify HR about performance concerns. Do not create
organizational employee analytics. The employee and manager control what gets shared outside
the platform.

1. My 1:1
2. Performance
3. Goals
4. Development
5. Career

7. History

Keep the interface clean and approachable. The product should feel like a modern conversation

## 3. My 1:1

The primary experience. A shared 1:1 workspace for both people.

Both users can add topics before the meeting. Categories: Wins, Priorities, Roadblocks,
Workload, Feedback, Recognition, Career, Development, Team issues, Questions, Support needed,
Other.

Each person adds "I want to discuss…". The other person can see the topic before the meeting.

**During the 1:1**, each topic can be marked: Discussed · Action Needed · Follow Up ·
Parking Lot · Resolved. Users can add notes and create action items directly from the
conversation.

**After the 1:1**, the platform summarizes: what we discussed, what we agreed on, actions,
who owns each action, due dates, topics to revisit.

---

## 4. Interactive employee check-in

A conversational check-in rather than a long form:

1. What are you most proud of since your last check-in?
2. What is going well?
3. What's getting in your way?
4. Where do you need more support?
5. What would you like to accomplish next?
6. Is there anything you want to discuss with your manager?

The system adapts based on responses. If the employee says "I'm overwhelmed with my
workload," it asks: *Which responsibilities are taking the most time?* then *What would help
most: reprioritization, additional support, clearer expectations, or something else?*

The goal is to help the employee arrive at the 1:1 prepared for a productive conversation.

---

## 5. Manager check-in

The manager's own preparation experience:

What is this employee doing particularly well? · What impact are they having? · What should
they continue doing? · Where could they improve? · What specific examples support that
feedback? · What support can you provide? · Are expectations clear? · What skills could they
develop next? · Is there an opportunity for a stretch assignment?

The system encourages specific, observable feedback. If a manager writes "Communication needs
improvement," the platform prompts: *Can you provide a specific example of when communication
created an issue?* This helps managers move from vague feedback to useful coaching.

---

## 6. Performance

### Achievements

Documented throughout the year. Categories: Business results, Customer impact, Collaboration,
Leadership, Problem solving, Innovation, Operational improvements, Team contribution, Other.

### Feedback (two-way)

- **Manager → Employee**: Recognition, Coaching, Performance feedback, Expectations,
  Development feedback
- **Employee → Manager**: What's working, What could improve, What support is needed, What
  would help the employee succeed

Feedback should be conversational and constructive.

---

## 7. Goals

Created collaboratively. Each goal contains: Goal · Why it matters · Success measure · Owner ·
Target date · Progress · Obstacles · Manager support · Status.

Statuses: Not Started · In Progress · At Risk · Complete · Deferred.

Goals are revisited during 1:1s. Example nudge: *"You haven't updated this goal in three
weeks. Would you like to discuss progress during your next 1:1?"* This must not feel punitive
— the purpose is accountability and support.

---

## 8. Learning & development

A major feature. Managers can recommend; employees can also request.

Types: Course · Training · Coaching · Mentoring · Job shadowing · Stretch assignment · New
project · Conference · Certification · Reading/resource · Peer learning · Leadership exposure ·
Cross-functional experience.

A development recommendation captures: Development Area · Why it matters · Recommended
Activity · Manager Support · Target Date · Success Measure.

**Example**
Development Area: Executive Presentation Skills
Why: Increase effectiveness presenting to senior stakeholders
Recommended Activity: Present monthly business updates
Manager Support: Review the first two presentations and provide feedback
Success Measure: Confidently lead quarterly leadership presentations

---

## 9. Career development

**Employee prompts**: Where would you like your career to go? · What type of work interests
you? · What skills would you like to build? · What experience would help you move toward your
next role? · Are you interested in managing people?

**Manager prompts**: What strengths do you see? · What capabilities should this employee
develop? · What experiences could help them grow? · What responsibilities could they take on
next?

The platform helps turn the conversation into a development plan. It does not promise
promotions or make automated promotion decisions.

---

## 10. Performance concerns

Managers can document concerns privately within the manager-employee workspace. The system
guides toward objective documentation:

What happened? · When did it happen? · What expectation was not met? · Was the expectation
communicated? · What specific examples exist? · Has this been discussed previously? · What
support was provided? · What outcome are you seeking?

Encourage factual, behavior-based documentation rather than assumptions about intent.

> Replace "They don't care about deadlines."
> with "The project was due August 5. The deliverable was submitted August 9 without advance
> communication about the delay."

The platform must not make legal conclusions or diagnose employee behavior.

---

## 11. Action items

Every conversation can create an action. Each contains: Action · Owner · Due date · Status ·
Related goal/topic · Notes.

The system surfaces overdue or upcoming actions without creating unnecessary pressure.

---

## 12. Conversation history

A chronological history of 1:1s, goals, feedback, achievements, development, career
conversations, and action items. Searchable and filterable by: 1:1 · Performance · Goals ·
Development · Career · Feedback · Actions.

---

## 13. Performance review preparation

**Employee** summarizes: major accomplishments, goals completed, goals still in progress,
feedback received, development completed, career discussions, challenges, future goals.

**Manager** summarizes: employee achievements, goals, feedback, development, areas of
strength, development opportunities, examples discussed throughout the year.

The system creates a draft summary the user can edit. Nothing automatically becomes an
official performance rating.

---

## 14. Export

Critical feature. The employee or manager selects information and exports it.

**Options**: Current 1:1 · Selected 1:1s · Goals · Development plan · Performance summary ·
Action items · Selected conversation history · Full employee-manager record.

**Formats**: PDF, Word document.

Before exporting, show:

> You are about to create a file containing this information. You control who you share it
> with.

The platform never automatically sends the file to HR. The user downloads the file and decides
what happens next.

---

## 15. Privacy & security

Privacy is prominent throughout. Display a simple message such as:

> Private between you and your manager. Nothing is automatically shared with HR.

Do not expose information to other managers or employees. Do not create company-wide
dashboards, employee rankings, or automatic performance scores. Do not automatically identify
"high performers." Do not automatically notify HR. Do not automatically send performance
concerns outside the employee-manager relationship. Users should always know when information
is shared and who can see it.

---

## 16. AI conversation design

The AI acts as a **conversation coach**, not the decision-maker.

**It should**: ask useful follow-up questions · identify missing context · help users clarify
thoughts · turn vague feedback into specific examples · suggest conversation topics · suggest
development activities · help create measurable goals · summarize conversations · identify
agreed actions · help prepare for difficult conversations.

**It should not**: make employment decisions · determine performance ratings · recommend
termination · diagnose employees · make promotion decisions · make compensation decisions ·
make legal conclusions · automatically report concerns to HR · pretend to know facts the user
has not provided.

When appropriate it should say: *"I don't have enough information to make that
determination."*

---

## 17. Tone

Human · supportive · practical · professional · conversational · non-judgmental.

Avoid corporate HR jargon, robotic language, and overly formal performance-review language.

| Instead of | Use |
| --- | --- |
| "Please provide evidence demonstrating competency attainment." | "What did they do that shows this?" |
| "Identify developmental opportunities." | "What would help this person grow?" |
| "Document performance deficiencies." | "What specifically isn't meeting expectations?" |

---

## 18. Dashboard

The home screen immediately shows: your next 1:1 (date/time, topics to discuss, number of open
topics) · goals and progress · active development goals · upcoming actions · recent
conversations (most recent 1:1 and performance conversations).

Simple and useful rather than overloaded with metrics.

---

## 19. Key product principle

The platform answers five questions after every meaningful conversation:

1. **What happened?** — the conversation
2. **What matters?** — the key issues, achievements or goals
3. **What do we need to do?** — actions
4. **How will we grow?** — development
5. **When will we talk about it again?** — follow-up

Performance management should feel like an ongoing relationship and conversation, not a
once-a-year administrative exercise.

---

## 20. Core product differentiator

The central experience is:

**Prepare → Talk → Reflect → Act → Follow Up**

Instead of asking employees and managers to complete another form, the platform guides them
through the conversation. The ultimate goal is to make managers better at managing people, and
employees more comfortable owning their performance and development.
